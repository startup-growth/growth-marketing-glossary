Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the work of helping people find useful pages through organic search. It combines understanding what searchers need, making the right pages available to search engines, and earning enough trust and relevance to compete.

A job board with hundreds of listings

Suppose a niche job board has 600 performance marketing jobs but rarely ranks for the searches that matter. Its national category page lists jobs from across two countries. Meanwhile, search results for people in New York are full of pages specifically about local openings.

Adding another generic paragraph about what performance marketing means is unlikely to solve the mismatch. The job board could instead build a New York page that shows current local listings, meaningful filters, salaries where available and clear links to actual job descriptions.

The page still needs genuinely relevant inventory. Publishing a page for every town with no local jobs creates little value.

Diagnose before changing the site

Start in Google Search Console. Look at queries and pages separately. Which pages get impressions? Are rankings concentrated in a few cities or devices? Are the important category pages indexed and canonicalized as expected?

Then inspect the actual search results for the query. A search for “performance marketing jobs” needs available positions. A search for “performance marketing definition” needs an explanation. A page targeting the wrong intent can be perfectly optimized technically and still fail to satisfy the searcher.

Check whether job listings are reachable through ordinary HTML links, whether relevant locations have useful landing pages, and whether users can filter without losing the results they came to see.

What to measure

Separate three questions: did Google discover and index the pages, did the pages receive impressions and rankings for relevant queries, and did visitors actually apply or use the product?

Technical fixes matter when there is a technical problem. When pages are indexed but outranked by stronger or more relevant competitors, improve the experience, distribution and evidence of usefulness instead of repeatedly rewriting title tags.
