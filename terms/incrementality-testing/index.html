Experiments and measurement

Incrementality Testing

Incrementality testing measures how many extra outcomes advertising actually caused. A dashboard conversion is an attribution claim. Incremental conversions are the additional purchases, leads or customers that would not otherwise have occurred.

The retargeting campaign everyone loved

A hypothetical retailer spends heavily on retargeting people who already visited its checkout. The ad platform reports 500 purchases at an attractive cost.

The marketing lead suspects many of those buyers would have returned anyway. They split eligible people into a treatment group that can see retargeting ads and a randomized holdout that cannot.

Suppose each group contains 10,000 eligible people during the same period:

  • Treatment group: 120 purchases.
  • Holdout group: 100 purchases.

The observed difference is 20 incremental purchases per 10,000 people, before accounting for statistical uncertainty or design issues. Crediting the campaign with all 120 treatment purchases would confuse attributed outcomes with additional outcomes.

If the incremental contribution from those 20 purchases is less than campaign spend, the program may not be economical, even if its platform ROAS looks excellent.

What can go wrong?

The groups need to be comparable. If one group receives a simultaneous coupon, the result is contaminated. If people in the holdout see ads through another account or device, treatment leaks into control. A tiny number of purchases may make the estimate too noisy to use.

Sometimes you cannot randomize people directly. A geographic experiment can compare suitably matched regions, but seasonality and local differences need careful treatment.

What decision comes out of it?

A useful test helps answer how much to spend, on which audience, or whether the campaign should run at all. It may justify reducing branded search spend, redesigning retargeting or shifting investment toward acquiring people who did not already intend to buy.

Attribution reports remain useful for operational diagnosis. They should not be mistaken for causal evidence.

Reference: Google Ads on Conversion Lift.
