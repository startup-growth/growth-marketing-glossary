Growth Loops

A growth loop happens when using or growing a product helps bring in the next set of users. The output of one cycle becomes an input to the next. Invitations, shared work, customer-created content and reinvested revenue can each form loops, but only when the connection really occurs.

An invitation loop, with numbers

Suppose 200 active teams use a collaboration tool. In a month:

  1. Each team sends an average of three invitations.
  2. Half the invitations are accepted.
  3. Sixty percent of invitees complete the product's first useful task.

That produces 200 × 3 × 50% × 60% = 180 newly activated people.

The 180 matter only if some become active enough to invite more people later. If almost everyone accepts an invitation, views a document once and disappears, the product has an invitation feature rather than a durable growth loop.

A marketer or product manager would track invitations per active team, acceptance, activation, retention and time between cycles. They might discover that invitations sent from inside an unfinished shared task activate far better than generic “invite a friend” emails.

Find the actual reinvestment step

Content marketing can also form a loop, but publishing articles every week is not one by itself. A plausible loop would be: customers publish useful templates, those templates attract search traffic, some visitors become customers, and some of those customers publish new templates.

Try drawing the mechanism on paper. Put a customer action on the left, a result on the right, and an arrow showing how that result creates the next customer action. If there is no credible arrow back, it is probably a sequence of tasks.

Funnels answer a different question

The funnel for our invitation example shows how many invitations are sent, opened, accepted and activated. The loop asks whether those activated users create additional invitations. You need both to diagnose why growth is or is not compounding.

A high invitation rate can also be bad if users feel pressured or invited people never see value. Include retention and customer experience in the assessment.
