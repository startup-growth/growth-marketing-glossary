Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO)

Conversion rate optimization (CRO) improves the percentage of eligible visitors who complete a useful action on a website or product. The action has to be worth something to the business. A form submission from someone you cannot serve may not be a useful conversion.

The landing page that “improved” and got worse

Consider a hypothetical B2B landing-page test. Both versions receive 2,400 eligible visitors:

  • Original page: 160 demo requests, of which 80 meet the sales team's qualification rules.
  • New page: 170 demo requests, of which 55 are qualified.

The new version gets more form submissions, a relative increase of 6.25%. But it produces 31.25% fewer qualified requests. A report focused only on form completion would recommend the wrong page.

The numbers are illustrative and the result would still need statistical analysis. The important lesson is to select the right primary outcome before testing.

Look at what the visitor needs

If someone clicks a search ad for payroll software, the page should answer practical questions: Does it support their country? What does it cost? Does it integrate with their accounting software? How much work is involved in switching?

A glowing testimonial cannot compensate for an unanswered eligibility question. CRO work can involve changing the offer, explaining a product constraint earlier, simplifying a form or improving mobile performance.

Review real sessions and customer questions before drawing a new design. Then choose a specific hypothesis, such as “showing supported countries above the form will reduce unqualified demos.”

Measure beyond the button

Use qualified conversions ÷ eligible visitors when lead quality is central. Check source, device and new versus returning visitors. Make sure repeated form submissions do not count as distinct customers.

An A/B test helps separate the impact of the page from changes in traffic mix. Activation and CAC can reveal whether a higher-converting page actually improves the broader funnel.
