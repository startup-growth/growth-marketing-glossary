Growth strategy and frameworks

AARRR Framework

AARRR is a checklist for the stages through which a business gets and keeps customers: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral and Revenue. Dave McClure popularized it as “pirate metrics.” Its value is that it stops teams from treating every signup as a successful customer.

Put actual events behind the five letters

For a hypothetical appointment-booking marketplace, an analyst might use:

  • Acquisition: a first-time visitor lands on a provider page.
  • Activation: the visitor completes a first appointment.
  • Retention: the same customer books again within a relevant period.
  • Referral: an existing customer brings in someone who completes an appointment.
  • Revenue: the marketplace receives its fee for a completed booking.

Notice that a person viewing a provider profile is not activated, and an invitation email is not a completed referral.

Now imagine the marketplace gets 8,000 new visitors and 800 account registrations, but only 120 first appointments. Its activation problem is more immediate than its referral program. The team could inspect provider availability, booking friction and cancellation rates.

Use the framework in an actual review

Open your weekly dashboard and ask what changed at each stage, using matching time windows where appropriate. If registration jumps 40% after a new campaign while first appointments remain flat, look at who the campaign attracted and what happened after registration.

Then put a decision beside the finding. For instance: stop optimizing for cheap registrations, and change campaign reporting to cost per completed first appointment.

Where AARRR runs out

Not every business moves through these stages in a neat order. Someone can pay before experiencing full value; a customer may never refer anybody. AARRR also cannot establish that an ad or onboarding change caused the outcome.

Use funnels to measure a specific sequence, incrementality experiments for causal impact, and retention cohorts for repeat behavior.
