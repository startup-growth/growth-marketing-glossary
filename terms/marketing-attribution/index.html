Marketing Attribution

Marketing attribution is how analytics systems assign credit for a conversion to the channels a customer touched. It helps explain paths to purchase, but different attribution rules can credit the same sale differently.

One customer, several dashboards

A hypothetical buyer reads an unpaid search article, later clicks a Meta retargeting ad, then searches the brand on Google and completes a $200 purchase.

Depending on their measurement windows and models, the ad platforms may both report a conversion. An analytics report may credit the last eligible non-direct touchpoint, while another model distributes credit across steps.

There was still only one $200 purchase. Adding platform-reported revenue together can overcount the same money.

Read the attribution settings

Before making a budget decision, inspect:

  • Which actions count as conversions, and whether duplicate events are removed.
  • Lookback windows for clicks and views.
  • Whether the model is last click or data driven.
  • Whether direct visits and cross-device paths are represented.
  • Whether a paid platform's reporting is being compared with actual orders or CRM customers.

In Google Analytics, attribution reports can show different credit under data-driven and last-click models. Changes in consent, tagging or window settings can also move reported channel totals without changing actual sales.

What should you do with the numbers?

Use paths to form hypotheses. If branded search appears at the end of many journeys, it may be harvesting demand created earlier. If a content page repeatedly introduces qualified buyers, it could be worth improving even if last-click conversions are low.

For large budget shifts, use incrementality testing to estimate what advertising added. A retargeting campaign that takes credit for purchases that would happen anyway has low causal impact even if attributed ROAS is high.

For channel-level planning, marketing mix modeling can assess aggregate patterns. Keep a separate view of real orders, revenue and acquisition cost so models do not become mistaken for the underlying business.

Reference: Google Analytics on attribution models.
