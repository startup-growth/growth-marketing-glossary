Customer Lifetime Value (CLV/LTV)

Customer lifetime value (LTV or CLV) is an estimate of what a customer will contribute over their relationship with a business. You need to specify whether you mean revenue, gross profit or contribution after additional service costs.

Work the numbers before comparing channels

Consider a hypothetical subscription service charging $100 per month. Assume monthly customer churn stays near 5%, average revenue is stable, and customers do not expand or downgrade.

A common shortcut gives revenue LTV ≈ $100 ÷ 0.05 = $2,000.

If gross margin is 65%, the rough gross-profit LTV is $1,300. The service does not keep all $2,000 in revenue as profit.

The shortcut relies on a fairly steady churn pattern. It is much less useful for a business with annual contracts, large expansions, seasonal purchases or dramatic first-month churn.

Check the estimate against real customers

Export cohorts by the month customers joined. For each cohort, sum revenue and cost of service in months one, two, three and onward. That shows how much value has actually been earned before adding a forecast of future months.

Suppose paid search customers often cancel after their trial discount, while referred customers remain for a year or more. One blended LTV number will conceal the difference. Compare LTV by acquisition source only when sample sizes are meaningful.

How to use it in a budget decision

If a customer costs $400 to acquire, a forecasted $1,300 in lifetime gross profit might look attractive. But if most of that profit arrives years later, the company still needs enough cash to fund acquisition now. Calculate payback time as well.

Use CAC definitions that match the LTV population and cohorts that show what customers actually did.
