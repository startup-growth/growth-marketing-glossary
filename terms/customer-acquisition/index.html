Acquisition and channels

Customer Acquisition

Customer acquisition means winning new customers. In a marketing report, that word can refer to visitors, leads, trial accounts or actual paying customers. Name the event. Otherwise, different teams will report different “acquisition” numbers for the same campaign.

Two channels, one misleading cost comparison

A hypothetical subscription company spends $6,000 on each of two campaigns:

  • Channel A brings 30 new paying customers, a media cost of $200 each.
  • Channel B brings 20, a media cost of $300 each.

Channel A looks better. Three months later, however, only ten A customers remain subscribed, compared with sixteen B customers.

The right comparison depends on the revenue and margin produced by those cohorts. Channel B might be the better investment despite the higher upfront cost. The team should compare matched customers by acquisition source and age, not just initial conversion counts.

What to do in the ad account and CRM

First, agree on a new-customer event. A lead form submitter is not a customer. Join campaign tracking to customer records so you can see which people activated, purchased, renewed or churned.

Next, divide the results by channel, offer, geography and customer segment. Check whether each channel attracts the audience the business can actually serve. A campaign may appear cheap because it generates customers who fail eligibility checks or require expensive support.

Then examine the entire cost. Media spend is a useful channel diagnostic; fully loaded CAC may also include staff, agency and sales costs.

Attribution is a separate issue

A conversion credited to paid search might have happened after someone was already convinced by an article or personal recommendation. For large spend decisions, an incrementality test can help assess what additional customers advertising actually produced.

Performance marketing often manages paid acquisition. SEO attracts search demand without paying for each click. Retention determines whether those wins last.

Before scaling any channel, get one report that shows spend, new customers, activation and later contribution from the same cohort.
