Demand Generation

Demand generation builds interest among potential buyers before and during their decision to purchase. In B2B marketing, it may involve research, educational content, webinars, paid distribution and coordinated follow-up. The goal is a healthier stream of potential customers, not simply more form submissions.

A buyer who isn't ready for a demo

Consider a hypothetical payroll software company selling to businesses with 50 to 500 employees.

A finance manager reads an article about payroll errors. She attends an online session about multi-state compliance, shares it with her HR lead, and only two months later searches for software vendors.

A campaign measured only by same-day demo requests will miss much of that journey. But calling every article view “pipeline influenced” is also unhelpful. The company needs to know whether it reached the right buyers and whether real buying interest developed.

Build a practical program

Choose a specific audience and problem. For example, target finance leaders at employers opening offices in a second state. Publish a useful explanation of the payroll complications, distribute it where those leaders pay attention, and create an easy path to evaluate the product when the problem becomes urgent.

Then connect the work to a small set of measurements:

  • Whether the intended accounts engaged with the information.
  • Qualified conversations with buyers who have the relevant problem.
  • New opportunities and eventual revenue over a realistic buying window.
  • Incremental results from paid distribution when a valid experiment is feasible.

A huge list of low-fit ebook downloads may be worse than a small number of serious buying conversations.

Distinguish different jobs

Performance marketing may specialize in capturing demand efficiently through paid channels. SEO can bring buyers to relevant explanations and product pages. Demand generation connects those activities to a broader buying process.

Talk with sales about which questions customers ask before a deal. Use those questions to improve the content, the website and follow-up messages. If the audience is wrong, adding more nurture emails will not fix it.
