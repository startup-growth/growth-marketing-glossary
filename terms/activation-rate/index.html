Activation and retention

Activation Rate

Activation rate is the percentage of new users who reach a meaningful first result. A signup, an app open or a click on “Get started” may be easy to track, but those events often say little about whether the customer received value.

What counts as activation in a CRM?

A hypothetical CRM gets 1,000 new accounts this month:

  • 480 import at least one contact.
  • 290 record a genuine opportunity or customer interaction.
  • 210 invite a teammate.

Should importing contacts be the activation event? Maybe. But importing an empty or unusable CSV does not help a team manage relationships. A stronger candidate could be recording a usable interaction against a real contact.

Under that definition, activation is 290 ÷ 1,000 = 29% within the stated measurement window. The team still needs to investigate whether that event predicts later retention.

Inspect the failed path

Follow accounts that did not activate. Are people abandoning contact imports because the CSV format is confusing? Do they finish importing but never see where to record the next action? Did the signup promise something the product does not deliver?

Check session recordings, failed event logs and support messages. Then watch a few new users try the workflow. If 200 accounts fail an import validation step, fix the validation problem before sending everyone another onboarding email.

Measure the change correctly

Suppose an improved import flow gets activation from 29% to 35% in the next cohort. It looks promising, but the cohorts might differ by acquisition source or company size. A controlled A/B test would give stronger evidence if the traffic supports one.

Also watch time to first value. An account that reaches its first useful result in ten minutes has a different experience from one that gets there after two weeks.

Activation connects the funnel to retention. Pick the event carefully, document it, and resist changing its definition every time a dashboard needs to look better.
