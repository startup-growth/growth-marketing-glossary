Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) measures how much a business spends to win a new customer. Paid platforms often show a narrower cost per conversion. That number is useful for managing campaigns, but it may leave out staff, agencies and sales costs.

The difference between ad CAC and fully loaded CAC

A hypothetical company acquires 100 new paying customers in a month. Its acquisition costs are:

  • Google and Meta advertising: $15,000.
  • Agency fees: $3,000.
  • Allocated sales and marketing staff: $5,000.

Its simple fully loaded CAC is ($15,000 + $3,000 + $5,000) ÷ 100 = $230.

The ad dashboard might report $150 per acquired customer, because it includes only media spend. Neither number is inherently wrong. They answer different questions.

If the business has a long sales cycle, spending this month may produce customers next month or next quarter. Align cohorts and costs before treating $230 as a precise economic result.

Use it to diagnose campaigns

Break media cost per customer down by channel and audience. Then look at downstream activation, refunds and retention.

Suppose one campaign generates paying customers at $120 each, but half ask for a refund. Another costs $190 per paying customer but has almost no refunds. The first campaign can be substantially worse even though its ad account appears more efficient.

Also check whether a campaign is credited with customers who would have purchased anyway. Attribution assigns credit; incrementality investigates additional demand.

When to raise a budget

Compare fully loaded CAC with gross-profit LTV and payback period. If acquisition costs are climbing but qualified volume and contribution are not, pause the increase and investigate.

Organic search has costs too, including writers, engineers, tools and outreach. It simply does not charge an identical amount for every click.
