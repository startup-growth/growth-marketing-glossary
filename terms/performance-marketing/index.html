Performance Marketing

Performance marketing is the practice of running marketing programs against measurable results, usually in paid channels such as Google Ads, Meta Ads and LinkedIn Ads. Good performance work connects ad-platform metrics to customer quality and economics, not merely cheap clicks or leads.

A cheap lead that isn't cheap

A hypothetical B2B company compares two campaigns over the same period:

  • Meta lead forms: $2,000 spent, 100 leads, $20 cost per lead. Nine leads qualify for sales.
  • Google Search: $3,000 spent, 50 leads, $60 cost per lead. Thirty qualify for sales.

Meta appears three times cheaper on raw CPL. But its cost per qualified lead is $2,000 ÷ 9 = about $222. Search comes in at $3,000 ÷ 30 = $100 per qualified lead.

The next question is which leads actually become customers. Sales qualification is a useful middle step, not the final commercial result.

The work behind the report

Open the ad account and check search terms, audience exclusions, conversion settings and creative. Then open the CRM and follow each lead through qualification and sale.

For the Meta campaign, examine whether the lead form attracts people outside the company size or location that sales can serve. For Search, check whether high-intent keywords drive better customers at a sustainable cost.

On the website, inspect page speed, offer clarity and form errors. A landing-page problem can make an otherwise sensible channel look inefficient.

What to measure every week

Track spend, qualified pipeline, new customers, gross profit or contribution, and payback by channel. Use click-through rate and cost per click as diagnostics, not as the scorecard.

Attribution matters when several ads touch the same customer. Incrementality testing can help determine whether spend is generating new demand or taking credit for demand that already exists.

Growth marketing may extend into onboarding and retention, while a performance role often concentrates on acquisition. In real job descriptions, the boundary varies enormously.
