Growth strategy and frameworks

Growth Strategy

A growth strategy is a decision about where to spend scarce time and money to grow a business. It should tell a team which customer to pursue, which problem is limiting growth, and why a particular set of actions is more promising than the alternatives.

Choose one constraint to work on

A hypothetical B2B software company has a $25,000 monthly marketing budget. Its funnel looks like this:

  • 5,000 people visit the site.
  • 500 create trials.
  • 50 connect their first data source.
  • 20 become paying accounts.

The team could buy more traffic. But 90% of trial accounts never connect their data, which is necessary to experience the product. Before expanding acquisition, the growth lead asks support to review failed connections and watches ten onboarding sessions. Six users get stuck at the same permissions screen.

The next month's plan might be: fix the permissions explanation, reduce failed connections, and test an onboarding assist for accounts that stall. Keep the existing acquisition campaigns running, but do not double their budgets yet.

Turn a choice into a plan

A useful one-page strategy for this company would state:

  • Customer: operations teams at companies with 20 to 200 employees.
  • Current constraint: too few trials reach first value.
  • Bet: getting more accounts through data connection will raise paid conversion.
  • Work: improve the setup flow, add troubleshooting and instrument failure reasons.
  • Decision point: compare activation and paid conversion for new cohorts after the change.

Those are choices with a reason behind them. “Improve SEO, launch a newsletter and test LinkedIn Ads” is a task list with no priority.

When the strategy should change

If activation rises but paid conversion stays flat, investigate whether the product delivers enough ongoing value. If customers activate and pay but churn quickly, retention may be the real constraint.

A funnel shows where customers are lost, while unit economics shows whether fixing that loss is worth the investment. Revisit the strategy when the evidence changes, not simply because a new channel looks exciting.
