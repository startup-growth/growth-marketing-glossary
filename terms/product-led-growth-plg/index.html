Product-Led Growth (PLG)

Product-led growth (PLG) is a way of acquiring and expanding customers by letting them experience useful product functionality before a traditional sales process. It often involves free trials or free plans, but those are pricing choices. The important part is how people discover value and invite broader adoption through actual use.

What happens after someone signs up?

Consider a hypothetical collaborative design tool. An individual signs up, creates a board and shares it with two colleagues. One colleague contributes to the board. The third joins a week later after seeing the shared work in an email.

Now the product has three users inside one company, with no salesperson involved. If they keep collaborating and eventually need permissions or admin features, the company may have a reason to pay.

The growth team needs to follow that path:

  • Signup to first useful board.
  • First board to first collaborator.
  • Collaborator invite to accepted invite.
  • Active team to retained team.
  • Retained team to paid workspace.

A “free users” dashboard will not explain which step is failing.

Fix the product before buying more signups

Suppose 1,000 people start a trial, but only 110 create a board. One-third of those who create a board invite a colleague. The team may be better off improving the first-board experience than adding more traffic.

Watch onboarding sessions, ask what the first successful project looks like, and make that path clearer. Then measure whether activation and retention improve.

When sales still matters

PLG does not mean “no sales team.” A large customer may adopt the free product first but still need procurement reviews, security commitments and a negotiated contract. Product usage can create a qualified sales conversation.

Be careful with expansion metrics. An upgrade triggered by a frustrating artificial limit can raise short-term revenue and hurt long-term retention.

The feedback from shared boards and invitations resembles a growth loop if active users reliably bring other active users. The economics still need to work, which is where unit economics enters.
