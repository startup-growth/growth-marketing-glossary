# Conversion Rate

Conversion rate is the percentage of people who complete a defined action out of the people who had the opportunity to complete it. The action might be a purchase, a trial signup or a demo request. The denominator matters as much as the conversion count.

## The conversion rate that fell without anything breaking

A hypothetical software company gets traffic from two sources: people searching for its product by name and people reading general how-to articles.

In April, 800 visitors arrive from branded search and 80 sign up. Another 200 arrive from the articles and four sign up.

- Branded search: 80 ÷ 800 = **10%**.
- Articles: 4 ÷ 200 = **2%**.
- Overall: 84 ÷ 1,000 = **8.4%**.

In May, both sources convert at exactly the same rates. But the company gets only 400 branded visitors and 600 article visitors. That's 40 signups from branded search and 12 from the articles.

The overall rate falls to **52 ÷ 1,000 = 5.2%**, even though neither source performs any worse.

A weekly report might blame the website. The actual change is traffic mix.

## Count the people who could convert

Before calculating a rate, decide what belongs in the denominator. A paid checkout should not include existing subscribers who cannot buy the same plan again. A location-specific service may need to separate visitors it cannot serve.

Sessions and people are also different units. Someone who visits three times and buys once has a different session conversion rate from their user conversion rate. Mixing the two can produce a convincing-looking but meaningless trend.

For lead generation, check whether a submission is qualified. [Conversion rate optimization](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/conversion-rate-optimization-cro/) explains why more forms can produce worse business results.

## What to do when a rate changes

Break the number down by source, device, market and new versus returning users before making a design change. Check that the conversion event still fires correctly. If the same segment genuinely worsened after a release, investigate the affected journey.

An [A/B test](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/a-b-testing/) can help establish whether a proposed fix caused a change. For a trial product, follow signup rate through to [activation](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/activation-rate/) rather than celebrating registrations that never become useful accounts.
