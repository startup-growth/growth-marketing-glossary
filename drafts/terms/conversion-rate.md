# Conversion Rate

Conversion rate is the percentage of eligible people who complete a defined action, such as buying a product, requesting a demo, or activating an account. It only becomes useful when the action, audience, and measurement window are clear.

**Formula:** Conversion rate = (conversions ÷ eligible people) × 100.

## A landing page with two different conversion rates

A hypothetical software company receives 2,000 visits and 180 demo form submissions. Its visit-to-form rate is **180 ÷ 2,000 = 9%**.

But only 1,200 visitors are eligible for the product, and 72 submit qualified requests. Its qualified conversion rate is **72 ÷ 1,200 = 6%**.

The figures answer different questions. The first measures all form submissions relative to visits. The second measures qualified demand among eligible visitors. Neither rate is meaningful without its denominator.

Decide whether to count visits, users, or accounts. The same person submitting a form three times should not automatically count as three customers.

## Can a higher conversion rate be bad?

A shorter form can attract more submissions while reducing the percentage the sales team can serve. The page may appear to improve even as the business receives fewer qualified leads.

That is why [conversion rate optimization (CRO)](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/conversion-rate-optimization-cro/) starts with a useful action, rather than a button click. For a product trial, signup rate may matter less than later [activation](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/activation-rate/). For advertising, a flood of unqualified leads can raise [customer acquisition cost](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/customer-acquisition-cost-cac/).

## How do you compare conversion rates fairly?

Keep the event definition and denominator consistent. Compare traffic sources, device types, and new versus returning visitors. A campaign can send more ready-to-buy visitors and raise conversion rate without any site change.

To estimate whether a page change caused the improvement, use a properly designed [A/B test](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/a-b-testing/) when practical.
