# Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

Net revenue retention (NRR) measures the recurring revenue kept from an existing group of customers after upgrades, downgrades, and cancellations. New-customer revenue does not count.

**Formula:** NRR = (starting recurring revenue + expansion − contraction − churn) ÷ starting recurring revenue × 100.

## Can retention exceed 100% even when customers leave?

Imagine a hypothetical software company starts a quarter with $50,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) from its existing customers. Over the quarter, that same group produces:

- $8,000 in extra MRR from upgrades.
- $3,000 in lost MRR from downgrades.
- $4,000 in lost MRR from cancellations.

The ending MRR from those starting customers is **$50,000 + $8,000 − $3,000 − $4,000 = $51,000**.

NRR is **$51,000 ÷ $50,000 = 102%**. Some customers left, but expansion among the remaining customers more than offset their lost revenue.

If the company also signs $15,000 in new MRR, that money stays out of the calculation.

## What can a high NRR hide?

A handful of large accounts can expand rapidly while many smaller customers leave. NRR may look healthy even as the customer base becomes more concentrated and less stable.

Check customer retention, gross revenue retention, the number of expanding customers, and how much of the gain comes from price increases rather than additional usage.

Use a consistent measurement period and customer cohort. Reporting systems may treat customers who leave and reactivate differently, so document the method.

## How is NRR different from customer retention?

[Customer retention](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/customer-retention/) counts customers who stay. NRR measures revenue from the same customer group, including upgrades and downgrades.

NRR is useful when estimating [customer lifetime value](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/customer-lifetime-value-clv-ltv/), but it is not a profit measure. A company can retain more revenue while spending heavily to serve those accounts. Review [unit economics](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/unit-economics/) alongside it.
