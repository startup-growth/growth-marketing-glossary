# Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

Net revenue retention (NRR) measures how much recurring revenue a business keeps from the customers it already had. Upgrades add to the number. Downgrades and cancellations subtract from it. Revenue from brand-new customers is excluded.

## The company with 102% retention and unhappy customers

Suppose a hypothetical B2B software company begins a quarter with $50,000 in monthly recurring revenue from its existing accounts.

Over the quarter, that same group produces:

- $8,000 in additional monthly revenue from upgrades.
- $3,000 less from downgrades.
- $4,000 lost from canceled accounts.

The group ends the quarter generating **$50,000 + $8,000 - $3,000 - $4,000 = $51,000** per month.

NRR is **$51,000 ÷ $50,000 = 102%**. Expansion more than offset the revenue the company lost.

The business also signed $15,000 in new monthly subscriptions. That revenue is real, but it does not belong in NRR.

## Look at what the 102% hides

Before expansion, the original customers retained only **$43,000 ÷ $50,000 = 86%** of their starting recurring revenue. That's the gross revenue retention in this simplified example.

Now suppose nearly all $8,000 in upgrades came from one enterprise customer while many smaller accounts canceled or downgraded. The NRR headline still looks good. The company has become more dependent on one account and may have a broader retention problem.

A marketer should look at the number of expanding and shrinking customers, the segments losing revenue, and why the expansions happened. A negotiated price increase is different from customers paying for more seats because they use the product more.

## Decide what to do with the number

Keep the same starting customer group and revenue definitions across periods. Document how reactivated accounts are handled, since that choice can vary by reporting system.

[Customer retention](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/customer-retention/) counts who stays. NRR captures how their recurring revenue changes. Compare both with [cohort analysis](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/cohort-analysis/) before increasing acquisition spend.

Revenue retention can improve without the business becoming more profitable. [Unit economics](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/unit-economics/) and [lifetime value](https://startup-growth.github.io/growth-marketing-glossary/terms/customer-lifetime-value-clv-ltv/) help show what the retained accounts actually contribute.
